The problem

The failure you didn't hear coming.

Bearings and gearboxes don't fail all at once. They degrade quietly for weeks — and then let go in an afternoon. By the time a machine is loud enough or hot enough for a person to notice, the damage is done and the schedule is gone.

Monthly rounds with a portable data collector were designed for a different era. A reading every 30 days misses everything that happens in between — and one unplanned stop on a critical line costs more than years of monitoring it.

Under the hood

From the bearing to your browser.

  • The sensors talk to the gateway wirelessly — short-range, low-power radio built for exactly this job.
  • The gateway brings its own internet — a cellular link with an encrypted tunnel. Your firewall rules don't change, because nothing arrives at your firewall.
  • The platform does the heavy lifting — storage, FFT analytics, thresholds, and alerting run outside your plant, maintained by us.
Security teams love this architecture for what it doesn't do: it opens no ports, installs no servers, and never touches the plant network.

Deep diagnostics

Analyst-grade FFT, every stored point on screen.

Most dashboards decimate the spectrum to fit the screen and quietly throw away the resolution your analyst needs. Spectra-IO renders every stored point — up to 4,000 bins per axis, with native resolution kept where bearing analysis happens.

  • Harmonic ruler — anchor a base frequency and read its multiples straight off the spectrum.
  • Bearing fault frequencies — configure the bearing and running speed, and the platform knows where BPFO, BPFI, BSF, and FTF should appear.
  • Hz and CPM, zoom bands, axis switching — the workflow a vibration analyst actually uses, in a browser.
This is the difference between "the app shows a chart" and "the analyst can make the call from the chart."

Where it goes next

From the plant floor to the oil field.

Inside a plant, sensors sit close together and one gateway covers them. But a lot of critical equipment doesn't live in a plant — it's scattered across kilometers: well clusters, pump stations, remote skids.

That's a radio problem, and the platform is ready for it. With long-range LoRaWAN nodes, a handful of gateways can cover an entire field of dispersed assets — years of battery per node, no SIM card per machine, no trenching. The platform ingests standard MQTT either way: the radio is an interchangeable detail, not a new system.

Dispersed extraction assets — exactly what long-range, low-power radio was designed for.
